## The inspection sequence on the bench

The video follows a small formed component held on a fixture. The technician brings the indicator into contact with a face, checks the surface, then changes the part's orientation to inspect another area. The sequence also includes bench adjustment followed by another check.

That recheck is an important part of the process. If a forming adjustment is made, the part needs to be measured again in the agreed setup. For repeat production, the result should feed back into tooling and process control rather than depend only on how a sample feels in the hand.

For an order, link this kind of inspection record to the part number, drawing revision and batch. The measurement sheet should identify the feature being checked, the setup, the actual reading and the acceptance limit. That makes the record useful to a buyer who was not at the bench.

## Flatness, parallelism and fit are different questions

Buyers often describe a formed part as needing to be "flat" or "straight." Those descriptions help start a conversation, but the drawing needs to be more specific. Flatness concerns the form of a surface itself. Parallelism and perpendicularity concern its orientation relative to a datum. Formed height, bend angle and hole position may each have their own requirement as well.

An indicator can be used in different setups, so an indicator sweep alone does not define which characteristic is being accepted. The support points, reference faces, clamping and probe direction should match the drawing and the inspection method. This visit records the checking sequence; the exact characteristic and tolerance for a purchase order belong on its controlled inspection plan.

For a custom stamped bracket, it helps to explain the assembly first. Which face seats against another part? Which holes locate it? Does a flange carry a pivot, guide or cover? Those answers help the supplier choose sensible datums and avoid measuring an easy dimension while overlooking the one that controls fit.

## A practical metal stamping quality inspection checklist

For a new drawing-based order, agree the following points before samples or production are approved. This is a planning checklist, not a claim that every listed test was performed during the visit.

| Check area | Details to agree before ordering |
| --- | --- |
| Part and material identity | Drawing number, revision, steel or other metal grade, stock thickness and required documents |
| Cut profile and holes | Outside profile, hole and slot locations, edge distance and any specified gauging |
| Formed geometry | Bend angle, formed height, offsets and drawing-defined surface relationships |
| Edges and appearance | Burr limit, permitted edge break, scratches and working surfaces that must be protected |
| Secondary operations | Joining, machining, heat treatment or finish requirements included in the finished item |
| Assembly fit | Mating parts or fixtures, movement, clearance and agreed functional checks |
| Sampling and records | Lot definition, sample size, measuring method, results and handling of nonconforming pieces |

If heat treatment or finishing follows the forming stage, say which dimensions must be accepted afterward. If the order is a metal subassembly, include the assembled relationship between parts, not only the dimensions of each loose component.

The same clarity helps with quotation comparison. One offer may cover a deburred, treated and assembled part; another may cover only the stamping. Put both offers on the same finished-part basis before comparing prices.

## Small assemblies need their own checks

A stamped metal assembly introduces interfaces that do not exist in the blank alone. For a lever fitted with a sleeve, for example, the sleeve position and the relationship to the arm may matter to the mechanism. The required joining method, movement or retention should come from the design.

## FAQ

### What should an inspection plan cover for stamped metal parts?
A useful plan covers the drawing revision, material and thickness, critical dimensions, formed geometry, burrs, finish, assembly fit, sampling and required records. It should identify which features are accepted after secondary treatment or assembly.

### Does a dial indicator check always measure flatness?
No. An indicator can be used for different comparative and geometric checks. The drawing characteristic, support, datum setup, clamping and probe direction determine how the readings should be interpreted.

### Should stamped components be checked after bending and finishing?
Final assembly-critical dimensions should be accepted in the condition specified on the drawing. Where forming, treatment, finishing or joining can affect the required geometry, include the appropriate final-stage checks in the order plan.

### What information is needed to source custom stamped brackets?
Send the controlled drawing, material grade, sheet thickness, quantities, critical dimensions, edge requirements, secondary operations, inspection scope and destination. Mating-part information or a reference sample can help explain fit.
