We sourced an industrial chiller, a plastic pellet auto loader, MRO parts and hydraulic components for an overseas customer, then brought the purchases together for container shipment from China. The machines were packed in wooden cases, with the smaller goods in cartons.

This was a mixed purchasing order rather than a shipment of one product. The customer needed equipment as well as parts for the factory, so our sourcing work covered both. Bringing the goods together also meant planning how the machinery and smaller purchases would leave China as one order.

## A chiller and an auto loader in the same purchasing plan

The chiller and auto loader were the larger equipment items in this order. In plastics processing, a chiller supplies cooling for the process or associated equipment, while an auto loader transfers plastic pellets to a hopper or processing machine. For a buyer adding either machine to an existing operation, the connections and site requirements matter as much as the equipment name.

For a new order, we would start with the buyer's application and the equipment already on site. The following information helps suppliers quote the right configuration.

## The MRO and hydraulic parts were part of the same order

Alongside the machines, we sourced MRO items and hydraulic components for the customer. These smaller purchases are easy to overlook when most of the attention is on the equipment, but they still need the right specifications, quantities and packing.

For a mixed list, we ask buyers to provide complete part numbers or drawings where available and identify any items that are needed urgently. A hydraulic component needs its own model and connection details confirmed; an MRO item may need a size, material or electrical rating. That work belongs in the purchasing stage, before the items are grouped for shipment.

Once the purchases are ready, the packing list needs to connect each case or carton with the items inside. Separate accessories should have their own reference, so the customer knows which package to open when the equipment is installed.

For similar orders, we can coordinate supplier packing information and collection details as well as the sourcing itself. Keeping different models and sizes separate on the list makes it easier for the receiving team to find the right parts without unpacking the whole delivery.

## Planning the loading before the goods arrive

When purchases come from several Chinese suppliers, readiness dates matter as much as the collection address. One supplier may have cartons ready while another is still finishing a machine. The decision is whether to wait and ship together or release an urgent item separately.

Packed measurements help make that decision practical. A machine's catalogue dimensions are not its shipping dimensions once a wooden case and any separate accessory packs are included. Confirming those measurements early gives the loading team a better basis for arranging the cargo and the buyer a clearer basis for comparing shipment options.

The final arrangement also needs to take account of package weight, stacking limits, handling access and cargo securing. These are points to agree with the packing and transport teams for the actual load, rather than deciding that any carton can fill any remaining space.

## What consolidation changes for the buyer

With this order, the customer used MAVORIX to source both the equipment and the smaller parts, then bring them together for shipment. That is also how we can help with a broader factory purchasing list: follow the individual requirements while coordinating the order as a whole.

It is not always worth holding everything for one departure. An urgent replacement part may need to travel separately, and a late machine can change the plan for the rest of the goods. The useful comparison is the total arrangement: collection, any waiting or storage, packing, transport and the effect on the factory's schedule.
