During a factory visit, we took samples from a batch of finished door lock drive gears. These small stamped steel parts work inside the lock mechanism, where their profile and fit matter more than their size might suggest. By the time we handled them, they had already been heat treated, blackened and dipped in rust-preventive oil.

The production route for this order was SPCC low-carbon steel, stamping and forming, deburring, carbonitriding, blackening, then an oil dip. That sequence is worth discussing with a supplier early. A quotation for an unfinished stamping is not the same as a quotation for a finished part ready for assembly.

## A small lock component with several process requirements

For a door lock gear, the outside profile, central opening, thickness and any formed features have to work together. An edge that catches, a hole that does not fit its mating part or a distorted body can make assembly difficult even when the overall shape looks familiar.

This is why we treat custom metal stamping as a drawing-based purchase, not simply a request for a similar-looking item. The approved drawing should identify the working surfaces, the dimensions that affect assembly and the condition in which those dimensions must be accepted. A lock component is also part of a larger mechanism; any required operating-load or life testing belongs to the agreed component and assembly validation plan.

At the factory, we laid selected pieces out separately and took close views of the finished batch. The black finish and oil were already in place, so the sampling record captures the parts at the end of their processing route rather than immediately after the press.

## From SPCC stamping to a finished, oiled part

SPCC is a commercial-quality cold-rolled steel designation, as listed in [Nippon Steel's cold-rolled sheet and coil catalog](https://www.nipponsteel.com/product/catalog_download/pdf/U003en.pdf). For this order, it was the specified starting material. The grade, sheet thickness and supply condition should stay on the purchasing specification so that a repeat batch starts from the same agreed basis.

| Process stage | Its role in this order | What to put in the purchase specification |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stamping and forming | Produce the part outline, opening and formed geometry | Drawing revision, material thickness and critical dimensions |
| Deburring | Remove unwanted burrs from cut edges | Permitted burr height, edge condition and areas where rounding must be controlled |
| Carbonitriding | Provide the specified case-hardening treatment | Hardness requirements, case-depth definition, test locations and batch records |
| Blackening | Give the part its specified black finish | Blackening process specification and appearance requirements |
| Rust-preventive oil immersion | Leave a protective oil film before packing | Oil type, coverage, drainage and compatibility with later assembly |

Deburring needs a clear limit. Removing a sharp burr is useful; rounding a working edge beyond the drawing requirement is not. On small stamped components, the supplier needs to know which edges can be eased and which profiles must be preserved.

The same principle applies to dimensions. If a bore or formed height controls assembly, agree whether its tolerance applies after heat treatment and finishing. Measuring only the unfinished stamping leaves the later process stages outside that acceptance check.

## What carbonitriding does for stamped steel parts

Carbonitriding is a case-hardening process in which carbon and nitrogen diffuse into the steel surface, followed by hardening. It is used to develop a hard, wear-resistant surface on suitable steel parts. [Bodycote's carbonitriding overview](https://www.bodycote.com/what-we-do/precision-heat-treatment/case-hardening-atmosphere-vacuum/carbonitriding/) describes its use for small components and explains the role of the hardened surface layer.

For buyers ordering custom stamped steel parts with heat treatment, the important question is not just whether carbonitriding is included. It is what result the drawing requires: surface hardness, the defined depth of the hardened case, any core requirement and the final dimensional limits. Thin sections and small features make it especially important to review the treatment with the supplier and heat treater rather than copy a hardness value from another part.

For a new or repeat order, ask for treatment records tied to the production batch, together with the agreed hardness and case-depth results. Define the test method and location before production. These are separate acceptance records from the factory sampling photographs; the inspection plan should cover both the visible condition and the specified material properties.

## Blackening and oiling have a different job

The gears were blackened and immersed in rust-preventive oil after carbonitriding. The glossy appearance in the close-up comes from the finished, oiled surface. Carbonitriding addresses the required surface-hardening function; blackening and the oil treatment address the specified finish and protection during handling and storage.

Keep the blackening specification and the oil requirement separate on the RFQ. For a black-oxide finish, an appropriate oil can add corrosion protection, as explained by [Birchwood Technologies](https://www.birchwoodtechnologies.com/products/black-oxide/). The exact blackening process and oil used on an order should be confirmed with its supplier, rather than chosen by color alone.

For blackened steel parts with rust-preventive oil, buyers should also agree how the oil affects the next operation. Will the parts be assembled as supplied, cleaned first or used near grease, plastic or adhesive? The answer influences the oil choice, the amount left on the surface and the packing instructions. Any corrosion-test target needs its own agreed conditions and acceptance criteria.

## Planning the finished-part inspection

This visit recorded on-site sampling and the finished appearance. For a drawing-based order, a useful inspection plan goes further by matching each requirement to a suitable check. The table below is a purchasing checklist, not a list of test results from this visit.

| Inspection point | What the buyer and supplier should agree |
| --- | --- |
| Part identity | Drawing number, revision and batch identification |
| Working profile and edges | Tooth or drive profile, burr limit, edge damage and formed-feature condition |
| Final dimensions | Bore, thickness, formed height, flatness and other drawing-critical dimensions after processing |
| Heat-treatment result | Batch-linked hardness and case-depth records using the specified methods |
| Finish and oil | Appearance limits, oil coverage, cleanliness and corrosion requirement where specified |
| Assembly fit | Approved mating part or fixture, operating check and acceptance limit |
| Sampling and records | Lot definition, sampling method, sample size and treatment of any nonconforming pieces |

Agreeing these points before production is much easier than discussing them when cartons are ready. For example, a general visual sample and a functional fit sample may need different quantities and handling. If a piece is cleaned for measurement, its corrosion protection should be restored as required before it is returned to the batch.

## FAQ

### Can door lock gears be made by metal stamping?
Yes. The door lock drive gears in this case were produced from SPCC steel by stamping and forming, followed by deburring, carbonitriding, blackening and oiling. Whether stamping suits another gear depends on its drawing, material, thickness, working profile and required performance.

### Why use carbonitriding on low-carbon steel stampings?
Carbonitriding introduces carbon and nitrogen at the surface before hardening to develop a wear-resistant case. For a specific stamping, the material, section size, hardness, case depth and final dimensions need to be reviewed together by the buyer, supplier and heat treater.

### Is the black finish the same as the heat treatment?
No. For this order, carbonitriding was the heat-treatment stage. Blackening and rust-preventive oil immersion followed as separate finishing steps. The purchase specification should identify each process and its acceptance requirements.

### What should be checked after heat treatment and finishing?
A finished-part inspection plan should address the drawing-critical dimensions, working profile, edges, distortion, finish, oil condition and packing. Hardness and case depth need their own agreed test methods and batch-linked records, with assembly checks where required.
