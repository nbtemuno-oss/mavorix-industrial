## What the Romanian buyer needed

The buyer was producing a long tapered tube that could be used as a table leg, chair leg or similar metal furniture component. A standard hydraulic tube-end reducer was not automatically suitable because the reduced section in this drawing was unusually long. The machine needed enough working length and matching tooling to form a gradual profile rather than a short stepped reduction.

The supplied part drawing showed:

- Starting outside diameter: Ø25 mm.
- Tube wall thickness: 2 mm.
- Overall tube length: 450 mm.
- Long formed section: 400 mm.
- Small-end outside diameter: Ø12.5 mm.

The drawing was the basis for discussing machine capacity and mold design. It did not, by itself, specify material grade, diameter tolerance, roundness, surface finish or acceptable transition geometry. Those items need to be stated separately when the finished tube must fit another component or meet a visible furniture finish.

## Why we selected a ZS-60 tube tapering machine

The proposed equipment was a ZS-60. Overseas buyers use several names for this machine: tube tapering machine, pipe reducing machine, tube swaging machine and tube end forming machine. For this project, “tube tapering and reducing machine” was the clearest description because the required part had a long change in diameter rather than a threaded or cut end.

The supplier's ZS-60 document listed the following main parameters:

| Item | Supplied specification |
|---|---|
| Maximum processing capacity | Ø60 x 2 mm |
| Maximum reducing length | 420 mm |
| Cycle time | 5-25 seconds per cycle |
| Mold open/close stroke | 50 mm |
| Main motor | 5.5 kW TAIHU Y132M2-6 |
| High-speed bearing | NSK, Japan |
| Relay and AC contactor | CHINT, China |

The 420 mm reducing length was particularly relevant because the drawing called for a 400 mm formed section. Capacity still had to be considered as a combination of tube diameter, wall thickness, material strength, reduction ratio and mold profile. A maximum value printed in a specification sheet is not a guarantee for every material and shape.

## Matching the mold to the tube drawing

The machine document made one practical point clear: different tube sizes require different molds. This matters when a buyer has a family of furniture legs that look similar but use several starting diameters or finished profiles.

For each part, the supplier needs the starting tube, finished drawing and material details. A useful tooling review should cover:

- Original outside diameter and wall thickness.
- Welded or seamless tube construction.
- Material grade and hardness.
- Finished small-end diameter.
- Taper length and any straight reduced section.
- Transition radius at both ends of the taper.
- Diameter, roundness and surface tolerances.
- Whether the visible surface will be painted, plated or polished.
- Required production quantity and mold life expectation.

Without this information, two suppliers may quote machines that sound similar while including different tooling or making different assumptions about the finished part.

For this type of heavy metalworking machine, packing is more than closing a plywood box. The base should support the machine at its structural mounting points, and the machine must be fixed against rolling, sliding or tipping. Molds, tools and loose accessories should be separately identified and secured so they cannot strike the machine in transit.

Moisture protection, lifting points, center of gravity and unloading method also need attention. The buyer should know whether a forklift, crane or container unloading platform will be available at destination before the packing plan is finalized.

## What buyers should send for a pipe reducing machine quotation

The phrase “pipe reducing machine” is too broad for an accurate quotation. Buyers should send:

- Tube drawing and a photo of the finished part if one exists.
- Starting outside diameter, wall thickness and total length.
- Tube material grade, hardness and weld condition.
- Finished diameter, taper length and transition shape.
- Critical tolerances and inspection method.
- Number of different part sizes and required mold sets.
- Quantity per size and target pieces per hour.
- Site voltage, frequency and phase.
- Required controls, guarding and conformity documents.
- Spare tooling and wear-part requirements.
- Destination, unloading method and packing requirement.

This information helps separate a suitable long-profile tube tapering machine from a short-stroke tube-end former or another swaging process that does not match the part.

## FAQ

### What is a tube tapering machine used for?
A tube tapering machine reduces round tube diameter over a controlled length to form a cone, long taper or smaller cylindrical section. Typical applications include furniture legs, lighting parts, automotive tubes and components that need a reduced end for assembly.

### Is a pipe reducing machine the same as a pipe swaging machine?
The terms overlap in overseas purchasing. Swaging describes a cold-forming process used to reduce or taper a tube, while pipe reducing machine is a broader commercial description. The drawing and process matter more than the name alone.

### What is the maximum capacity of the ZS-60?
The supplied document lists Ø60 x 2 mm and a maximum reducing length of 420 mm. Actual suitability depends on material strength, starting size, reduction ratio, finished shape and tooling.

### Does every tube size need a separate mold?
The supplied ZS-60 document states that different tube sizes require different molds. A quotation should list all starting sizes and finished profiles so the tooling scope is clear.
