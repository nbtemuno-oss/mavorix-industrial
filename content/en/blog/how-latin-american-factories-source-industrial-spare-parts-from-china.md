## Common problems when buying spare parts from China

The most common problem is not only supplier reliability. It is unclear specification. A supplier may quote a part that looks similar but is not suitable for the machine.

Typical risks include:

- A part number is missing, incomplete, or copied incorrectly.
- The buyer sends only one photo without machine context.
- Voltage, frequency, pressure, stroke, thread, port size, or connector type is not confirmed.
- The quotation does not clearly state brand, model, material, origin, warranty limit, packing, or lead time.
- The supplier proposes a compatible replacement without explaining what is different.
- Several small orders are shipped separately, increasing cost and coordination work.

For technical items, similar appearance is not enough. A sensor, relay, bearing, hydraulic valve, pneumatic cylinder, belt, filter, heater, gearbox, motor, seal, or control component may have hidden differences that affect installation and service life.

## Why technical details matter before quotation

Before requesting a quotation, Latin American buyers should prepare a clear spare-part file. The file should help the supplier understand the part, the machine, and the application.

Important details include:

- Machine brand, model, serial number, and nameplate photos.
- Original part number, supplier code, or markings if available.
- Clear photos from several angles.
- Installed-position photos showing how the part connects to the machine.
- Dimensions, mounting holes, port size, thread, connector, shaft size, belt width, or other interface data.
- Voltage, frequency, power, pressure, flow, speed, temperature, material, and working medium where relevant.
- Required quantity, urgency, destination country, and packing expectations.

For electrical items, voltage and frequency are especially important. For hydraulic and pneumatic parts, pressure, port, stroke, seal, and mounting details matter. For packaging machine parts and injection molding machine spare parts, machine application and installation position can be just as important as the product name.

## How to reduce sourcing risk

A safer sourcing process starts before payment. Buyers should slow down enough to verify the supplier, clarify the quotation, and confirm the specification.

Practical checks include:

- Verify supplier identity and basic business consistency before sending payment.
- Compare quotations by specification, not only unit price.
- Confirm whether the item is original brand, equivalent brand, compatible replacement, or custom-made part.
- Ask suppliers to confirm model, material, voltage, pressure, dimensions, lead time, packing, and available documentation in writing.
- Request photos or videos before shipment when practical.
- Use inspection or quality coordination for higher-value or technically sensitive orders.
- Consolidate small orders when several suppliers are involved.

## Information to send before asking for quotation

A clear RFQ helps suppliers answer faster and reduces mismatched offers. Buyers should send:

| Information | Why it helps |
| --- | --- |
| Product name and part number | Gives suppliers a starting point |
| Machine brand and model | Helps confirm compatibility |
| Photos and videos | Shows shape, markings, installation, and condition |
| Dimensions and interfaces | Reduces the risk of a visually similar but wrong part |
| Voltage, pressure, material, or application | Confirms technical requirements |
| Quantity and urgency | Helps check stock and lead time |
| Destination country | Helps plan packing, export communication, and consolidation |
| Existing supplier links or quotations | Makes comparison more practical |

If the part is urgent, explain the production impact. If it is for routine maintenance, group related items into one list so suppliers and sourcing partners can compare options more efficiently.
