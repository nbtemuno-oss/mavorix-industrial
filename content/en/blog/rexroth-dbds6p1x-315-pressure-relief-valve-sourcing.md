## What we check before asking suppliers to quote

A message saying “I need this hydraulic valve” with one distant photo is usually not enough. Before approaching suppliers, we try to organize the information that will determine what is actually being quoted.

The most useful starting points are:

- Brand shown on the old component.
- Complete model or type code.
- Material number, MNR or part number.
- Clear photos of every label.
- Hydraulic symbol where visible.
- Coil voltage and connector details for solenoid valves.
- Port, mounting and interface photos.
- Equipment brand and model.
- Required quantity.
- Destination country and timing requirement.

For this Rexroth order, the visible DBDS6P1X/315 marking gave suppliers a precise search reference. It was still important not to invent performance details from the code or assume that another DBDS6 valve would be suitable because it looked similar.

When the old label is damaged, photos of the valve in its installed position can help. The buyer's maintenance team may also need to measure the mounting pattern, ports or body and confirm the hydraulic function from the machine documentation.

## Exact model, successor model or compatible alternative?

Supplier replies should make clear which of three things is being offered: the exact requested code, a current successor, or a compatible alternative.

Those are not the same commercial or technical proposition. If the code changes, the supplier should explain why and provide enough information for the buyer's engineer to compare function, pressure range, flow requirement, electrical configuration, mounting and connection details.

MAVORIX can organize this comparison and follow up missing documents, but we do not approve hydraulic compatibility on behalf of the buyer's engineering team. Final selection and installation remain technical decisions for qualified personnel who understand the machine and circuit.

This is especially important for pressure-control components. A model reference should not be treated as proof of the actual commissioned pressure setting. The required range, adjustment and safe commissioning procedure must match the equipment.

## Comparing multi-brand hydraulic valve quotations

When several suppliers respond, the lowest number on the quotation is not automatically the best offer. First confirm that the quotations cover the same product scope.

For each line, compare:

- Quoted brand and complete model code.
- Material number or part number where applicable.
- Exact item, successor or proposed alternative.
- New, surplus or other stated condition.
- Quantity and unit of sale.
- Available product and label photos.
- Stated origin and available traceability documents.
- Warranty terms from the quoted supply channel.
- Packing method and domestic delivery in China.
- Lead time and how it is calculated.

A product photograph can help confirm labels and visible condition, but it cannot by itself prove authorized-channel status or technical authenticity. If traceability, manufacturer warranty or authorized distribution is a purchasing requirement, it should be written into the inquiry and checked against the documents available for that specific offer.

## Packing hydraulic valves for shipment

Hydraulic valves are compact but can be heavy. Several valve bodies placed loosely in one carton may strike each other during handling. Adjustment parts, connectors, labels and machined surfaces also need protection.

The photograph from this order shows individually wrapped valves grouped inside a carton with paper cushioning. Before dispatch, packing should be reviewed against the number and weight of the components, the transport method and the buyer's requirements. Heavier shipments may need stronger internal separation or a wooden case.

When hydraulic parts are consolidated with electrical and mechanical spare parts, the packing list should keep model references and quantities easy to trace. Small coils, seals or connectors should not disappear among heavier valve bodies.

## Finding hydraulic valves when the old model is difficult

Some sourcing requests begin with a clean label and a current part number. Others begin with an obsolete machine, a faded plate or a component that has already been replaced once with an undocumented alternative.

In those cases, the work starts with identification rather than supplier search. Clear photos, dimensions, hydraulic symbols, equipment manuals and purchasing history can narrow the possibilities.
