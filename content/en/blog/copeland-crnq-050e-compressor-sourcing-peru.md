## Starting with the complete compressor model

## Replacement checks before ordering

For a compressor replacement, an exact-looking shell is not enough. The old compressor label, equipment model and system data should be compared before purchase.

The main checks include:

- Complete model and suffix.
- Refrigerant used by the existing system.
- Lubricant type and service history.
- Voltage, phase and frequency at the installation.
- Required capacity at the actual evaporating and condensing conditions.
- Suction and discharge line sizes and positions.
- Mounting footprint and available cabinet height.
- Start components, protection and control arrangement.
- Site rules for refrigerant, electrical installation and pressure equipment.

Cooling capacity is condition-dependent. A catalog value measured at one evaporating and condensing point should not be treated as the guaranteed result in another climate, coil selection or control setup.

## Why the Peru destination changes the purchasing review

Peru commonly uses both 50 Hz and 60 Hz industrial equipment depending on the facility and imported machine. The visible compressor label provides both frequency rows, but that does not remove the need to confirm the actual site supply and the HVAC unit design.

The buyer should also confirm which refrigerant is installed, whether it can be legally and practically serviced, and whether the replacement will be commissioned by a qualified refrigeration technician. A compressor should never be selected only because a seller describes it as “suitable for air conditioning.”

For compressor transport, the unit should remain upright, ports should stay protected and the base should restrain the mounting feet. Loose accessories need separate identification. Where sea freight or long storage is expected, the packing plan should also address moisture protection and the destination unloading method.
